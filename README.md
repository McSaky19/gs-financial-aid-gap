# The Columbia GS financial-aid gap: a revolving emergency fund with alternative-signal scoring

Author: TAPE Saky Caleb Betsaleel Eliezer . Written in September 2026 21; files are uploaded on the dates shown by GitHub. Not an official Columbia document.

## The problem
GS covered 63% of demonstrated need last year, against 100% at Columbia College and Engineering, without guaranteed housing. 72% of first-generation and low-income GS students report some form of housing insecurity (sources in sources.md). Existing support is discretionary: a committee review of 5 to 7 business days and non-repayable grants. No Columbia school runs a repayable revolving fund or a fast alternative-signal score.

## The proposal
A satellite emergency fund built on five pillars, each tied to a named precedent: structural isolation, signal-based scoring (rent regularity, employment stability, academic engagement), deliberate targeting of profiles that classic credit reads poorly, revolving capital with a first-loss guarantee sized on the expected shortfall, and light follow-up after disbursement.

## What the model shows (illustrative)
Assumptions: $300,000 initial capital, $1,500 average loan, default probability 25.1% (derived from five illustrative profiles), recovery 15%, guarantee covering 100% of expected loss.
- Without guarantee: capital falls to $88,500 after five years and the fund serves 651 students cumulatively, fewer each year.
- With guarantee: capital stays at $300,000, 200 students per year, 1,000 over five years.
- Pessimistic case (default 32.1%, recovery 5%, guarantee 75%): capital $201,806, 859 students.
- A sensitivity table gives year-5 capital by default probability and guarantee coverage.

## Limits
Weights, threshold, default and recovery rates are illustrative and not validated on real data. The default probability comes from three approved sample profiles. Repayment lag within the year is ignored. Regulatory points (ECOA, FERPA) are listed in the proposal.
